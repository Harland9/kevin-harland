Finding a critical path in four steps

A project network looks scary until you treat it as arithmetic. First, the forward pass: start at 0, add each task's duration, and when a task has several predecessors, take the largest finish time. Second, the backward pass: start from the project's finish time and subtract durations going back, taking the smallest start time when a task feeds several others. Third, slack is the latest finish minus the earliest finish. Fourth, any task with zero slack is critical, and a delay there delays the whole project.

A small example: task A (3 days) leads to B (4 days) and C (5 days), and both lead to D (2 days). The path A, C, D takes 10 days, and B has 1 day of slack.

Choosing a development model without guessing

Waterfall, iterative, prototyping, spiral, V-model and Agile all cover the same phases: planning, requirements, design, coding, testing, deployment and maintenance. They differ in how easily you can change your mind along the way.

If requirements are clear and stable, Waterfall is simple and well documented. If the client keeps changing things, Agile's short sprints cope better. If risk is the main worry, Spiral reviews it in every loop. My exam rule is to name the model first, then give two or three reasons taken from the scenario.

Reflection 2: Group work and Tuckman's stages

Describe your own group, stage by stage

Forming

The group meets, people are polite, and roles and goals are unclear.

How was your group formed? Did you know your group mates beforehand? What was the first meeting like?

Storming

Ideas and working styles clash, and disagreements appear about roles and workload.

Were there disagreements or challenges? How did you resolve them?

Norming

The group agrees on rules, roles and routines, and trust grows.

How did the group develop shared expectations (meeting times, task split, communication channel)?

Performing

The group works effectively, and members support each other to reach the goal.

Did you reach this stage? Describe what it looked like in practice.

Adjourning

The task ends and the group closes, ideally with reflection and closure.

How did the group conclude? Was there closure, or unfinished tension?

Study Tips

Strategies I use, and the free tools that go with them.

Strategies

Write exam-style notes

After each lecture I cut the slides down to short, accurate notes that still cover most of the content, organised around likely exam questions.

Explain it in your own words

I close my notes and explain the topic as if teaching a friend, then check what I missed. If I can't explain it, I haven't learned it yet.

Practise with small tasks

For subjects like JavaScript and project scheduling, I work small examples by hand before moving on to bigger ones.
